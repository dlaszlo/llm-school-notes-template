# Learning-task-led visual design: research rationale

Prepared on 2026-09-28 from the user-requested Astra xhigh research and adopted by Codex into [media workflows](media-workflows.md) and [reusable prompts](media-prompts.md). This is a design rationale, not a separate operating system or evidence that bots are already deployed. Apply only the relevant prompt stage; do not load these papers for each generation.

* Visual usefulness depends on the task. Hegarty reviews how representation can support the operations the viewer needs to perform and how consistent notation helps across views. This supports selecting a form from the learning task, not ranking one infographic form as universally best. [Hegarty, 2011, The Cognitive Science of Visual-Spatial Displays](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1756-8765.2011.01150.x).
* Multiple representations may complement one another, constrain misinterpretation or support deeper understanding, but the learner also needs to relate them. Mixed forms can be useful; adding more views is not itself a benefit. [Ainsworth, 2006, DeFT: A conceptual framework for considering learning with multiple representations](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222410911_DeFT_A_conceptual_framework_for_learning_with_multiple_representations_Learning_and_Instruction_16_183-198).
* Place concise explanatory labels near the relevant visual element and make priorities clear to reduce unnecessary search. Findings about narration should not be transferred mechanically to static images. [Mayer and Moreno, 2003, Nine Ways to Reduce Cognitive Load in Multimedia Learning](https://carpentries.github.io/instructor-training/files/papers/mayer-reduce-cognitive-load-2003.pdf).
* Arrows can change what a diagram is understood to explain. In two experiments with mechanical diagrams, arrows affected structural versus functional explanations. Treat arrows as meaningful: distinguish part pointers, ordering markers and substantive relationships, and verify actual direction and endpoints. [Heiser and Tversky, 2006, Arrows in comprehending and producing mechanical diagrams](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1207/s15516709cog0000_70).
* Interesting but instructionally irrelevant details can harm learning in the studied settings. This does not establish a blanket ban on rich illustration. Keep scene details that help identify lesson concepts distinct from spectacle unrelated to the explanation. [Harp and Mayer, 1998, How Seductive Details Do Their Damage](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232595492_How_Seductive_Details_Do_Their_Damage_A_Theory_of_Cognitive_Interest_in_Science_Learning).

The open repertoire in the workflow is our design synthesis, not a scientifically exhaustive taxonomy. OTHER/ELSE is intentional: recognize the actual task, justify an appropriate custom/mixed representation, or choose no new image. Topology is a conditional tool for relationship tasks, not a requirement for every infographic. Exact diagrams and quantitative charts retain checkable code/vector foundations.

These studies do not directly test the selected image model, Hungarian generated learning images or the repository's learners. They provide design guidance, not guaranteed learning gains or guaranteed first-attempt generation quality. Source fidelity, direct visual inspection, actual reading-size checks, learner feedback and bounded repair remain necessary.

## Cross-subject and design supplement, 2026-09-28

A user-requested Astra xhigh follow-up compared the current learner subject lists with selected language, mathematics and professional curriculum passages. It identified a gap in the earlier topic-index survey: formal linguistic analyses and several mathematical/quantitative representations needed explicit precision coverage beyond technical drawings. The shared guidance therefore selects by the visual's meaning and precision needs, while allowing useful supplementary infographics in every subject. The full learner-specific inventory and source evidence remain in private planning records, not in the public template.

The follow-up also made contextual style, viewpoint, controlled comparison, accessibility and implied visual claims explicit design decisions. A Claude Fable 5.1 high document review sharpened the preservation definition: a generative redraw of an exact reference is not preservation. Classroom notation, existing coverage records and the learning role of a simplified overview remain identifiable. These are design judgments and review findings, not a new empirical study or a full curriculum/image audit. The resulting operational guidance is in [banner and infographic design decisions](media-workflows.md#banner-and-infographic-design-decisions) and the relevant [prompt stages](media-prompts.md); no additional checklist or mandatory corpus read is introduced.
