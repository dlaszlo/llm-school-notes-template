# Visual policy

Shared operating rules, loaded through [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md). Named rules in other modules are located through its rule map.

## Choosing and replacing authored visuals

* **Choose by learning function.** Keep editable SVG/code diagrams for exact geometry, vectors, force directions, dimensions, mathematical/formal structures, linguistic analyses, graph/set relationships, formulas and other precision-dependent content. Keep simple Mermaid/SVG where it is clear and the existing SVG page banners. For explanatory overview infographics, prefer the selected image generator when it materially improves visual explanation and readability. This is not a subject-based or blanket SVG-to-PNG conversion: historical maps and flow arrows also carry exact claims. The current selected infographic model is `openai/gpt-image-2.5-sunburst` through OpenRouter; its availability, authorization and spending limit must be established in the actual environment before paid calls. Missing access does not block the supported lesson or justify replacing a good existing image with a worse placeholder.
* **Preserve precision-dependent teaching diagrams.** Treat every precision-dependent teaching diagram in the notebook or teacher material to learn as lesson content, including mathematical/formal structures, linguistic analyses, quantitative graphs and technical drawings. Reproduce its instructional content faithfully in a precise, editable representation such as SVG, code or typeset notation, preserving the notation taught in class, distinct cases and their source order, meaningful orientation, labels, symbols, relationships, geometry, scales and conditions as applicable. A banner or generated infographic does not replace it. Compare the source diagrams with the resulting page through the existing two-way self-check coverage record; do not create a parallel ledger. Check an unclear or apparently incorrect feature against the source and retain a labeled correction or open question; do not invent it or silently omit it. No subject is excluded from useful infographics: a page may combine precise diagrams with a generated infographic when they serve distinct useful learning tasks. Preservation requires retaining the independently editable, deterministically rendered diagram and making it available on the page. A generator’s redrawing never counts as preservation, even when supplied with an exact reference. For a composite, assemble the precise layer from that authored source without generative redrawing, compare it after assembly and retain a link to the separate precise diagram.
* **Adapt visual style to meaning.** Choose the banner/infographic's visual language from the supported topic and relevant context, such as period, place, application or the world of a literary work, rather than imposing one look by subject name. Reuse coherent styling within a related topic or series when helpful. Preserve legibility, consistent semantic colors and notation, and meaningful distinctions without relying on color alone. Materials, clothing, objects and emotional tone must not introduce unsupported reconstruction, anachronism or distorted interpretation. Do not present a generated scene as an authentic source photograph, document or artwork. Prefer simpler illustrative detail where evidence is insufficient. The concrete choices belong in the existing image plan and provider prompt under [media workflows](../instructions/media-workflows.md#banner-and-infographic-design-decisions), not a new mandatory subject taxonomy.
* **Verify meaningful visual details with the appropriate method.** Across SVG, CAD, generated pictures and animation, identify which geometry, notation, relationship or motion carries the teaching claim. Check the actual constructed result against its source and applicable parameters/conventions, then inspect the final rendered view. A successful render or plausible appearance does not verify a gear profile, thread spacing, wall symbol, graph or other precise claim. Use the [visual selection guide](../instructions/technical-visuals.md) for context-sensitive representation/tool choice, a concrete drawing specification, faithful fallbacks, relevant checks and animation limits; preserve the existing source coverage and evidence record.
* **Choose examples with a visible reason to care.** A banner or explanatory illustration should use a recognizable, realistic problem or contrast that makes the taught relationship matter. Physical correctness alone is insufficient: compare plausible alternatives and choose the one that makes the benefit or distinction easiest to understand without contrived behavior or assumed specialist experience. The finished image must support that reading; do not infer a lesson that is only present in the prompt. Keep a banner uncluttered and leave calculations and precise constructions to the page.
* **Choose form from the learning task.** Select a visual from what the learner needs to recognize, explain, compare or do, the nature of the content and the actual reading size. Not every infographic is a relationship diagram; plan topology only when relationships matter to the learning task. The [visual selection guide](../instructions/technical-visuals.md) provides an open, capability-based repertoire: if none fits, justify a custom or mixed representation, reuse a suitable visual or choose no new image. Subject name, orientation or a previously successful example alone does not determine composition.
* **Replace selectively and verify.** For a requested improvement, inspect the actual current rendering, all affected page uses and the supporting evidence. Record keep, improve-svg or replace-with-png with a reason in the relevant evidence record. Generate a replacement into a new file while keeping the old image and links. Only after direct content, label, arrow/condition and phone-width checks pass, update all affected image links, alt text, captions, prose, descriptions and evidence-index entries together. A prettier image with lost conditions is not an accepted replacement. For print media also apply the specified A4 check. No mass replacement or paid regeneration is implied by adding this rule.
* **Describe the actual image for later agents.** Beside each newly generated raster infographic embed, add an HTML comment labeled `image-description` with its relative asset path (or stable external artifact ID), actual SHA-256, learning role, observed semantic description including the key labels, meaningful relationships, arrow directions and conditions, evidence-record link and limitations. Write it after directly checking the final image; the generation prompt describes intention and must not be substituted for observation. The evidence record holds the detailed labels, nodes, arrow directions, conditions, sources and verifier/time. Keep repeated uses consistent with that record. A changed hash or interpretation requires a new check and synchronized description. The comment aids navigation and text processing; it never replaces direct image inspection for a visual claim or correction. Visible alt text, caption and prose still carry the learning meaning. Comments are readable in source and must contain no secrets or restricted personal data.
* **Preserve evidence and appropriate storage.** Teacher images stay private evidence and must not be embedded; use independent visuals or independently acquired reusable originals under *Images from material to learn* in [Sources and evidence](sources-and-evidence.md). Acquired evidence remains unchanged; a generated lookalike cannot replace a real object used for recognition. Small authored lesson images may stay in `wiki/assets/` with relative links for offline and review access. Large standalone media follows the configured private external-media policy; do not assume a Drive share or image-hosting link is already available. Keep previous authored versions when needed to reconstruct evidence. Do not copy or trace prohibited textbook images through a generator.
* **Generate during ingest as well as on request.** The ingest role (Drax or equivalent) assesses the current lesson's visual needs and can execute the same infographic workflow as the media role (Mantis or equivalent): select scope and format, prepare the exact content and prompt, generate, directly inspect, repair within limits, embed and document. A separate Mantis conversation is not required. When an authorized ingest includes illustrations and a configured spending allowance is available, this is part of that ingest, without another per-image confirmation. Both roles use the same selected image model, prompt contract, evidence checks, image-description comments and bounded retry policy; the agent's reasoning effort need not be identical. This rule specifies the required capability, not proof that live tools or credentials are already configured.
* **Decide before drawing.** For relevant lesson sections, choose no new image, reuse an appropriate checked asset, precise SVG/code, or a generated infographic according to the learning goal and actual reading size. Explain meaningful choices briefly in the existing ingest/evidence record, not a separate catalogue. Do not add an infographic to every section. In statics, for example, a conceptual overview may use a generated image while a force construction retains precise editable geometry; a subject name alone never selects or prohibits a format. Existing-image replacement still requires the full replacement checks above.
* **Respect one scope and budget.** Both roles preserve the current lesson's core, prerequisites, selected depth and exact assertions. Use at most the first generation plus two repairs per banner or infographic and a configured whole-request spending cap covering all its images and paid checks; changing role, filename or resuming the work must not reset counters. Reuse an already accepted asset when the source version and intended use still match. At the limit, select the best usable checked candidate, never automatically the last. Cosmetic imperfections may remain with a recorded limitation; a materially false, misleading or unreadable draft cannot become finished teaching material. A specific further-call exception preserves the same counters and requires an explicit authorized number and budget. A failed or unavailable optional infographic must not prevent delivery of supported notes; retain a suitable existing asset or use an accurate simpler diagram and report the limitation. If a visual is essential to understand an unresolved claim, leave that part explicitly incomplete rather than declaring the lesson complete. No standalone podcast, presentation or printable packet is generated automatically merely because Drax is ingesting a lesson.
